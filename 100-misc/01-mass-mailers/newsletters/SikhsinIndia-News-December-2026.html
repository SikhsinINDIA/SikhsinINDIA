Founder’s message

Tavinder Pal Singh Arora

Founder, SikhsinIndia.com

Dear {{name}},

Thank you for welcoming the first issue of the SikhsinIndia.com newsletter into your home, and for sharing it with your family and friends.

December brings us to the month of Poh, the most solemn time in the Sikh calendar. It begins with the Gurgaddi of Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji and the Shaheedi of Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Sahib Ji, Bhai Mati Das Ji, Bhai Sati Das Ji and Bhai Dyala Ji, who gave their lives in Delhi for the freedom of others to follow their own faith.

In the week of the Safar-e-Shahadat we remember how Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji left Sri Anandpur Sahib, how His family was separated at the Sarsa, how the elder Sahibzade attained martyrdom at Chamkaur Sahib, and how the Chhote Sahibzade and Mata Gujri Ji gave their lives at Sirhind. In just a few days, the Guru gave His entire family for the Panth.

These are stories every Sikh child should know. I warmly invite you to read them with your children and grandchildren this month, to visit the pages listed in this newsletter together, and to keep alive the memory of those who never bowed before injustice.

As always, your suggestions, corrections and contributions are most welcome.

Bhul Chuk Maaf.

Saturday, 12 December · Gurgaddi Diwas

Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji

In 1675, when the Kashmiri Pandits came to Anandpur Sahib seeking protection, the nine-year-old Gobind Rai told his father, Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Sahib Ji, that none was more worthy than He to stand up for them. After His father’s martyrdom in Delhi, Gobind Rai became the Tenth Guru.

From that young age He carried the Guruship with extraordinary wisdom and courage, and in 1699 He created the Khalsa. On His Gurgaddi Diwas, let us draw strength from His example to stand firmly for truth and for the rights of others.

Sunday, 13 December · Shaheedi Diwas

Bhai Mati Das Ji, Bhai Sati Das Ji & Bhai Dyala Ji

Bhai Mati Das Ji, his brother Bhai Sati Das Ji and Bhai Dyala Ji accompanied Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Sahib Ji to Delhi in 1675. At Chandni Chowk, before the Guru’s own eyes, they were tortured and martyred for refusing to give up their faith.

Bhai Mati Das Ji was sawn in two, Bhai Sati Das Ji was wrapped in cotton and set alight, and Bhai Dyala Ji was boiled alive — yet each remained calm, reciting Gurbani to the end. Their names are remembered in the daily Ardaas of every Sikh.

Gurudwara Sis Ganj Sahib →  ·  The Greatest Shaheedi →

Monday, 14 December · Shaheedi Diwas

Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Sahib Ji — Hind di Chadar

Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Sahib Ji, the Ninth Sikh Guru, gave His life at Chandni Chowk, Delhi, in 1675 to defend the freedom of the Kashmiri Pandits to practise their own faith. He refused to convert or to perform a miracle to save Himself, and was martyred where Gurudwara Sis Ganj Sahib stands today.

Bhai Jaita Ji carried the Guru’s sacred head to Anandpur Sahib, and Bhai Lakhi Shah Vanjara cremated His body by setting fire to his own home, where Gurudwara Rakab Ganj Sahib now stands. A Guru who laid down His life for the faith of others is lovingly remembered as Hind di Chadar — the Shield of India.

On His Shaheedi Diwas, let us honour His sacrifice by standing up for the freedom and dignity of every human being.

Monday, 14 December · Prakash Purab

Sahibzada Baba Fateh Singh Ji

Sahibzada Baba Fateh Singh Ji, the youngest son of Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji, was born at Sri Anandpur Sahib in 1699, the year the Khalsa was created.

Though the youngest of the Chaar Sahibzade, he showed courage beyond his years. Barely six years old, he stood with his brother Baba Zorawar Singh Ji in the court of Sirhind and refused to give up Sikhi. On his Prakash Purab we celebrate the life of the youngest martyr of the Panth.

Monday, 14 December · Gurgaddi Diwas

Panj Piare — the Guru Khalsa at Chamkaur Sahib

At Chamkaur Sahib, with the fortress surrounded, Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji placed the Guruship in the Khalsa. Five Singhs, standing as the Panj Piare — the Guru Khalsa — then commanded the Guru to leave so that He could carry on guiding the Panth.

Guru Sahib, who had bowed before the Panj Piare to receive Amrit in 1699, again bowed to the will of the Khalsa. It remains a powerful reminder of the Sikh principle that the Guru dwells in the Khalsa.

Monday, 21 December · Historical

Leaving the fort of Sri Anandpur Sahib

After a long siege of Sri Anandpur Sahib by the Mughal forces and the Hill Rajas, the besieging armies swore solemn oaths on the Quran and on cows that the Guru and the Sikhs could leave in safety. In December 1704, on a cold winter night, Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji left the fort with His family and Sikhs.

The oaths were broken almost at once, and the Sikhs were attacked as they left. This night marks the beginning of the Safar-e-Shahadat — the journey of martyrdom — remembered by the Sangat every year in the month of Poh.

Tuesday, 22 December · Historical

The family separates at the Sarsa river

Pursued by the enemy, the Sikhs reached the Sarsa river in flood. Fighting a rearguard battle, many Singhs gave their lives, and in the confusion of the crossing the Guru’s family was separated.

Mata Gujri Ji and the two younger Sahibzade went one way; Mata Sundar Kaur Ji and Mata Sahib Kaur Ji reached Delhi; and Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji, with the two elder Sahibzade and about forty Singhs, made His way to Chamkaur. The family would never be together again.

Tuesday, 22 December · Shaheedi Diwas

Bhai Jivan Singh Ji (Bhai Jaita Ji)

In 1675 Bhai Jaita Ji brought the sacred head of Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Sahib Ji from Delhi to Anandpur Sahib at great risk to his life. Embracing him, the young Guru Gobind Rai blessed him with the words “Rangrete Guru ke bete” — the Rangretas are the Guru’s own sons.

Baptised as Bhai Jivan Singh, he became a renowned warrior of the Khalsa and attained martyrdom fighting for the Guru during the battles of December 1704.

Wednesday, 23 December · Saka Chamkaur Sahib · Shaheedi Diwas

Sahibzada Baba Ajit Singh Ji & Sahibzada Baba Jujhar Singh Ji

At Chamkaur Sahib, about forty Singhs held a small mud fortress against a vast army. Sahibzada Baba Ajit Singh Ji, aged about seventeen, asked his father for permission to go out and fight, and Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji blessed him and sent him into battle.

After his elder brother attained martyrdom, Sahibzada Baba Jujhar Singh Ji, aged about fourteen, followed him onto the battlefield and also gave his life. The Guru watched both His sons fall and thanked Waheguru that they had fulfilled their duty.

Thursday, 24 December · Shaheedi Diwas

Bhai Sangat Singh Ji

Bhai Sangat Singh Ji closely resembled Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji. When the Guru Khalsa ordered the Guru to leave Chamkaur Sahib, Guru Sahib placed His own kalgi and attire on Bhai Sangat Singh Ji, who stayed behind in the fortress.

Taking the enemy’s attention upon himself, Bhai Sangat Singh Ji fought to the end and attained martyrdom alongside the remaining Singhs — a sacrifice that allowed the Guru to continue His mission.

Monday, 28 December · Shaheedi Diwas

Sahibzada Baba Zorawar Singh Ji, Sahibzada Baba Fateh Singh Ji & Mata Gujri Ji

After the separation at the Sarsa, Mata Gujri Ji and the Chhote Sahibzade were betrayed and handed over to Wazir Khan, the governor of Sirhind. They were held in the open, freezing Thanda Burj, where Bhai Moti Ram Mehra risked his life to bring them milk.

Brought before the court day after day, Sahibzada Baba Zorawar Singh Ji, aged about nine, and Sahibzada Baba Fateh Singh Ji, aged about six, refused every promise and threat to give up Sikhi, answering with “Jo Bole So Nihal, Sat Sri Akal”. They were bricked alive and martyred, and on hearing the news Mata Gujri Ji too left her mortal body in the Thanda Burj.

Diwan Todar Mal bought the land for their cremation by covering it with gold coins. Gurudwara Sri Fatehgarh Sahib stands there today. As we remember the supreme sacrifice of the Chhote Sahibzade and Mata Gujri Ji, may their courage give us the strength to remain steadfast in our faith.

SECTION 03

Safar-e-Shahadat on SikhsinIndia.com

The Safar-e-Shahadat — the journey of martyrdom — is the most moving chapter of Sikh history. In the space of a few days in December 1704, Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji sacrificed His four sons and His mother for the Panth, and countless Singhs gave their lives beside them.

Many families observe this week with special simplicity: sleeping on the floor, avoiding celebrations, and gathering for Kirtan, Katha and Paath in remembrance of the Sahibzade and Mata Gujri Ji.
