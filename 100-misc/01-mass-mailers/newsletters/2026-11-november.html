SECTION 01

Founder’s message

Tavinder Pal Singh Arora

Founder, SikhsinIndia.com

Dear {{name}},

It gives me immense joy to share with you the very first newsletter of SikhsinIndia.com.

SikhsinIndia.com is more than just a website for me; it is a passion, a heartfelt commitment, and a legacy that I hope will endure for generations to come. My vision is to build a lasting reference, resource library, and encyclopaedia of Sikh heritage for the younger generation. In today’s fast-paced world, with changing lifestyles, modern aspirations, and countless opportunities, many young Sikhs find it challenging to remain closely connected with their faith and heritage. I hope that whenever they wish to learn about our Gurus, our history, or our Gurbani, they will find a trusted and welcoming resource here.

SikhsinIndia.com began with a simple yet meaningful aspiration: to bring our history, Gurbani, and Gurudwaras together in one place, accessible to every Sikh family and every friend of the Sikh community, in the language they feel most comfortable with.

Today, the website brings together the lives and teachings of the Ten Sikh Gurus and Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji; information on more than 600 historical Gurudwaras across India; Sikh history for every day of the year; the daily Hukamnama from Sri Harmandir Sahib; and online access to Nitnem, Sehaj Path, Sukhmani Sahib, and Akhand Path Sahib — in English, Hindi, and Punjabi.

November holds special significance in our Sikh calendar. It is a month of profound remembrance, devotion, and celebration, marking Bandi Chhor Divas, the Gurgaddi of Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, the Joti Jot of Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji, the Shaheedi of Baba Deep Singh Ji, and the Prakash Purab of Sri Guru Nanak Dev Ji.

I warmly invite you to explore these stories in this newsletter, share them with your family and friends, and help pass our rich heritage on to the next generation. Your suggestions, corrections, contributions, and stories about your local Gurudwaras are always welcome. Together, we can make SikhsinIndia.com a richer and more meaningful resource for the entire Sangat.

This journey has only just begun, and I am grateful to everyone who believes in its purpose and shares this vision.

Bhul Chuk Maaf.

Waheguru Ji Ka Khalsa, Waheguru Ji Ki Fateh!

With heartfelt regards,
Tavinder Pal Singh Arora
Founder, SikhsinIndia.com

Tuesday, 3 November · Gurgaddi Diwas

Sri Guru Har Krishan Sahib Ji

Born in 1656, Guru Har Krishan Sahib Ji, the Eighth Sikh Guru, was only five years old when He was bestowed with Guruship in 1661 — the youngest of the Sikh Gurus. His life reflected the divine attribute of Purity, showing that spiritual wisdom and compassion are not bound by age; His understanding astonished even learned scholars.

In Delhi, when an epidemic afflicted the city, Guru Sahib devoted Himself to serving and comforting the sick without distinction of caste, creed or standing. The people lovingly called Him Bala Pir, the child prophet. On this Gurgaddi Diwas, let us serve those in need and keep our hearts pure and humble.

Tuesday, 3 November · Joti Jot Diwas

Sri Guru Har Rai Sahib Ji

Born in 1630, Guru Har Rai Sahib Ji, the Seventh Sikh Guru, guided the Sikhs from 1644 to 1661. His life reflected the divine attribute of Mercy — gentle and peaceful, with deep compassion for every living being — while He kept alive the Sikh warrior tradition established by His grandfather, Sri Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji.

His leadership combined devotion, compassion, preparedness and service, showing that true strength can live alongside peace and humility. On His Joti Jot Diwas, may we cultivate mercy in our daily lives and serve humanity with devotion.

Guru Har Rai Sahib Ji →

Tuesday, 3 November · Prakash Purab

Mata Sahib Kaur Ji — Mother of the Khalsa

Mata Sahib Kaur Ji is revered as the Mother of the Khalsa. At the creation of the Khalsa in 1699 she was given the sacred honour of adding sweetness — pataase — to the Amrit prepared by Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji.

Embodying grace, strength and devotion, she remained a guiding presence for the Panth after the martyrdom of the Sahibzade. On her Prakash Purab we honour her motherly devotion to the entire Khalsa Panth.

Sunday, 8 November · Celebration

Bandi Chhor Divas

Bandi Chhor Divas — the Day of Liberation — commemorates the release of Sri Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji from Gwalior Fort and His return to Sri Amritsar. True to His compassionate nature, Guru Sahib refused freedom unless the 52 kings imprisoned with Him were freed too.

Guru Sahib wore a cloak with 52 tassels so that each king could hold one and walk out to freedom beside Him. That is why Sri Harmandir Sahib is lit with lamps every year on this day, coinciding with Diwali. May we carry His example of compassion, justice and selfless leadership, bringing light and liberty to all.

Bandi Chhor Divas →  ·  Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji →

Wednesday, 11 November · Gurgaddi Diwas

Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji (Sri Hazur Sahib, Nanded)

At Sri Hazur Sahib, Nanded, Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji bestowed eternal Guruship upon Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, ending the line of living Gurus and establishing the scripture as the eternal, living Guru of the Sikhs.

From that day the Sikhs look to the Shabad — the Divine Word — for guidance, so that the Guru’s wisdom remains forever accessible and unchanging for every generation. Let us bow before our eternal Guru and recommit ourselves to living by its teachings.

Takht Sri Hazur Sahib →  ·  Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji →

Saturday, 14 November · Joti Jot Diwas

Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji

Born in 1666, Guru Gobind Singh Ji became the Tenth Guru in 1675 after the martyrdom of His father, Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Sahib Ji. In 1699 He created the Khalsa and gave the names Singh and Kaur, uniting the qualities of the saint and the soldier — devoted to the Divine, ready to stand against injustice.

Guru Sahib gave everything for righteousness: His father, His mother and His four sons all attained martyrdom. His Zafarnama to Emperor Aurangzeb remains a powerful expression of moral courage. In 1708 at Nanded He bestowed eternal Guruship on Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, whose wisdom continues to guide us.

Guru Gobind Singh Ji →

Sunday, 15 November · Shaheedi Diwas

Baba Deep Singh Ji

Baba Deep Singh Ji is one of the most revered martyrs in Sikh history. At over seventy years of age, on hearing of the desecration of Sri Harmandir Sahib, he led a jatha of Sikhs to defend its sanctity.

According to tradition he fought on with extraordinary resolve even after being grievously wounded, reaching the parkarma of Sri Harmandir Sahib to pay his respects before attaining martyrdom. We remember his unshakeable devotion and courage in defence of the Guru’s house.

Shaheedi Diwas →  ·  Baba Deep Singh Ji →

Tuesday, 24 November · Prakash Purab

Sri Guru Nanak Dev Ji

The most joyous Gurpurab of the year celebrates the birth of the First Nanak in 1469 at Rai Bhoi Di Talwandi, now Sri Nankana Sahib. From an early age Guru Nanak Dev Ji illuminated the truth of One Creator, equality and compassion for all humanity.

Guru Sahib undertook four great journeys, the Udasis, across India and as far as Mecca and Baghdad, sharing the universal message of Ik Onkar, honest living and selfless service, and embracing people of every faith as one human family.

This Gurpurab, let us celebrate Guru Nanak Dev Ji’s eternal teachings — Naam Japna, Kirat Karni, Vand Chhakna — and recommit ourselves to the path of humility, equality and truthful living.

Guru Nanak Dev Ji – Gurpurab →  ·  Guru Nanak Dev Ji →

Saturday, 28 November · Remembrance

Bhai Mardana Ji merges with the Divine

Bhai Mardana Ji was the lifelong companion of Sri Guru Nanak Dev Ji and the player of the rabab. A devoted Muslim disciple, he accompanied the Guru on all four Udasis, across thousands of miles.

Through his music Bhai Mardana Ji gave voice to Guru Nanak Dev Ji’s divine hymns. His companionship remains a timeless symbol that devotion transcends every boundary of religion, caste and background.

Guru Nanak Dev Ji →

Monday, 30 November · Prakash Purab

Sahibzada Baba Zorawar Singh Ji

Sahibzada Baba Zorawar Singh Ji, the third son of Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji, was born in 1696 and raised under the loving care of his grandmother, Mata Gujri Ji, nurtured in deep faith from his earliest years.

At Sirhind, alongside his younger brother Sahibzada Fateh Singh Ji, he faced severe trials with remarkable courage and refused to abandon Sikhi despite unimaginable pressure — an eternal source of inspiration.

SECTION 03

Inside the Nitnem section

Nitnem — literally the “daily discipline” — is the heart of a Sikh’s day. The Nitnem section of SikhsinIndia.com gathers everything you need for daily Paath and for longer readings of Gurbani in one place, free for everyone.

Gurbani is a companion for every moment of life. A Sikh begins the day at Amrit Vela with Japji Sahib, Jaap Sahib and the Savaiye, pauses in the evening for Rehras Sahib, and closes the day with Kirtan Sohila — and in between, the Guru’s words are there when we need direction, strength, comfort or courage, as the Paath Reminders show. Even ten quiet minutes a day with Gurbani steadies the mind and keeps us connected to the Guru. Whether you are just beginning or have recited Nitnem for decades, these pages are here to help you read, listen and understand. The Nitnem page has eight buttons; here is what each one offers:

 
1 Mool Mantar — the root verse →

The Mool Mantar is the opening composition of Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, revealed by Guru Nanak Dev Ji, and the essence of Sikh belief. This page takes you through it word by word — Ik, Onkaar, Satnaam, Kartaa Purakh, Nirbhau, Nirvair, Akaal Moorat, Ajooni and beyond — explaining the attribute of the Divine that each word reveals. It is the ideal place to begin for anyone, young or old, who wants to understand what Gurbani teaches about the One Creator. Available in English, Hindi and Punjabi.

2 Ardaas — the Sikh prayer →

Ardaas is the prayer of supplication and gratitude that a Sikh offers, standing with folded hands, before and after every undertaking and at the close of every Path. In six short illustrated cards, this page explains its spiritual significance, its structure — from the remembrance of the Gurus and the sacrifices of the Panth to the prayer for Sarbat da Bhala, the welfare of all — and the blessings it brings to daily life.

3 Nitnem Path — PDF library →

All seven daily Banis, arranged by the time they are recited: Japji Sahib, Jaap Sahib, Tav-Prasad Savaiye, Chaupai Sahib and Anand Sahib in the morning, Rehras Sahib in the evening, and Kirtan Sohila at night. Each Bani has a short note on its meaning and significance, and a free PDF to download in Punjabi, Hindi or English — and for several Banis, all three together — so you can keep them on your phone or print them for the family.

4 Akhand Path Sahib Ji →

This page explains the beliefs, values and spirit of the Akhand Path, with answers to common questions. Use Send Request for Akhand Path Sahib Ji to sponsor a Path for a birthday, anniversary, new beginning or in loving memory; once it is approved, invite your family and friends. The complete Path then plays online, synchronised so that everyone hears the same Ang at the same moment wherever they are, and invitees receive reminders by email and WhatsApp an hour before the start, the Madh (Ang 705) and the Samapti. Use Participate in scheduled Akhand Path Sahib Ji to join a Path already in progress.

5 Sukhmani Sahib — the Psalm of Peace →

Composed by Sri Guru Arjan Dev Ji in Raag Gauri, Sukhmani Sahib brings peace of mind to all who recite it or listen to it. Sign in with your email or mobile number (creating an account is free) and the Paath resumes exactly where you left off at your last visit — so you can complete it in one sitting or a little each day. A companion page explains its meaning and its place in daily Sikh devotion.

6 Sehaj Path →

Sehaj Path is the complete reading of Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, from Ang 1 to Ang 1430, at your own pace — over weeks or months. Sign in and listen to the recorded Path; the site saves your place, down to the minute, so it resumes from where you stopped at your last login. Audio and video versions are both available, and an overview page explains how a Sehaj Path is undertaken and observed.

7 Shabad Kirtan Search & live Kirtan →

Watch the live Gurbani Kirtan broadcast from Sachkhand Sri Harmandir Sahib, Sri Amritsar, which runs from about 3 AM until night. To find a particular shabad, type a line in English letters, Gurmukhi or Hindi, or search by the kirtaniya (ragi jatha) — or both together — and listen right there on the page.

8 Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji — read and search →

Read every Ang, 1 to 1430, in Gurmukhi (with an optional Larivaar view), with transliteration in English and Hindi and meanings in English, Punjabi and Hindi. Go straight to any Ang, move Ang by Ang, and enlarge the text for easier reading. Search Gurbani by the first letter of each word, by words of a line, or by English meaning. The English translation is by Dr. Sant Singh Khalsa and the Punjabi teeka by Prof. Sahib Singh, via BaniDB.

9 Today’s Hukamnama →

Begin each day with the Guru’s word: the daily Hukamnama from Sri Harmandir Sahib, Amritsar, in Gurmukhi with its English meaning and the Ang it comes from, updated every morning. The Hukamnamas of recent days are kept there too, and on the homepage you can listen to the Hukamnama and its katha, recorded at Sri Harmandir Sahib.
