Ask a Sikh to name the most important gurudwaras and you'll often hear about the Takhts first. *Takht* means "throne": these are the five seats from which decisions affecting the whole Sikh community (the Panth) have traditionally been made. Together they are known as [the Panj Takhts](https://sikhsinindia.com/01-about-sikhism/01-05-panj-takhts/01-05-00-panj-takhts.html).

Visiting all five is a lifetime goal for many Sikh families. They are spread across four states, so the trip takes planning. Here's an overview.

## 1. Sri Akal Takht Sahib, Amritsar (Punjab)

**Founded:** 1606, by Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji.

Facing Sri Harmandir Sahib (the Golden Temple) across the same complex, the Akal Takht ("Throne of the Timeless One") is the oldest and highest of the five. Guru Hargobind Ji built it as a seat of temporal authority alongside the spiritual one: the principle of *miri* and *piri*.

**Visit tip:** Combine with the Golden Temple. Go before dawn to see the *Prakash* ceremony, when the Guru Granth Sahib is carried in procession.

## 2. Takht Sri Keshgarh Sahib, Anandpur Sahib (Punjab)

**Significance:** Birthplace of the Khalsa, Vaisakhi 1699.

Here Guru Gobind Singh Ji called for volunteers willing to give their heads, initiated the first Panj Pyare, and created the Khalsa. The festival of Hola Mohalla draws huge crowds here every spring.

**Visit tip:** About two hours by road from Chandigarh. Visit during Hola Mohalla for Gatka displays, but book accommodation early.

## 3. Takht Sri Damdama Sahib, Talwandi Sabo (Bathinda, Punjab)

**Significance:** Where Guru Gobind Singh Ji stayed in 1705–06 and where the final version of the Guru Granth Sahib was compiled. It's often called the *Guru ki Kashi*, a seat of learning.

**Visit tip:** Easily combined with a Bathinda trip. Vaisakhi is the busiest time.

## 4. Takht Sri Patna Sahib, Patna (Bihar)

**Significance:** Birthplace of Guru Gobind Singh Ji in 1666.

Patna also has several other historic gurudwaras linked to the Guru's childhood, such as Bal Leela Sahib and Kangan Ghat.

**Visit tip:** Patna is well connected by train and air. Allow a full day for the Takht and nearby gurudwaras.

## 5. Takht Sri Hazur Sahib, Nanded (Maharashtra)

**Significance:** Where Guru Gobind Singh Ji spent his final days and, in 1708, passed the Guruship to the Guru Granth Sahib.

The furthest from Punjab, [Takht Sri Hazur Sahib](https://sikhsinindia.com/01-about-sikhism/01-05-panj-takhts/01-05-05-hazur-sahib.html) sits on the banks of the Godavari and has its own distinct traditions.

**Visit tip:** There are direct trains from Amritsar and Delhi (look for the Sachkhand Express). Many families visit in November around Gurpurab.

## Planning a Panj Takht yatra

- **Route:** A practical order is Amritsar → Anandpur Sahib → Talwandi Sabo (all in Punjab, 3–4 days), then a train to Patna, then Nanded. Allow 10–12 days in total.
- **Stay:** Every Takht has a *sarai* (pilgrim lodging), usually free or nominally priced. Arrive early in peak season.
- **Langar:** Served throughout the day at all five.
- **Dress:** Head covered, modest clothing, shoes left at the counter.

The Takhts are more than landmarks. Each one marks a moment that shaped how Sikhs live today: miri-piri, the Khalsa, the Guru Granth Sahib as eternal Guru. Visiting them in sequence is a way of walking through Sikh history.
