Gurudwara Paonta Sahib Ji - Sirmaur District

Gurdwara Paonta Sahib is a revered Sikh shrine situated in Paonta Sahib, Sirmaur district, Himachal Pradesh, India.
It holds profound historical and spiritual significance as the residence of Guru Gobind Singh Ji, the tenth Sikh Guru, from 1685 to 1689.
During his stay, Guru Gobind Singh Ji composed several important works, including parts of the Dasam Granth, and established the Kavi Darbar, a court for poets and scholars.
The Gurdwara is located on the banks of the Yamuna River, which forms the boundary between Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand


The name "Paonta Sahib" is derived from the word "Paonta," meaning "foot," symbolizing the place where Guru Gobind Singh Ji's horse's hooves left an impression upon stopping there.
The site was gifted to the Guru by Raja Medini Prakash of Nahan, and Guru Gobind Singh Ji constructed a fort-like residence, which later evolved into the Gurdwara complex

During his time at Paonta Sahib, Guru Gobind Singh Ji engaged in various literary and martial activities.
He composed several writings, including parts of the Dasam Granth, and established the Kavi Darbar, a court for poets and scholars, fostering a rich cultural environment


The Gurdwara complex encompasses several significant sites:
• Sri Talab Asthan: A place where Guru Gobind Singh Ji distributed salaries to his soldiers.
• Sri DastarAsthan: A site where the Guru judged turban-tying competitions, emphasizing the importance of the Sikh identity.
• Kavi Darbar: The court where poetic symposia were held, fostering literary and scholarly pursuits
• Museum: Displays artifacts, including weapons and pens used by Guru Gobind Singh Ji, offering insights into the era's culture and the Guru's contributions
