The Sikh Calendar & Historical Dates

Sikh Calendar - Nanakshahi

Until the 13th of March, 1998, the Sikhs used a Hindu lunar calendar to determine their feast days[cite: 13]. They now use their own Nanakshahi calendar which started on 14 March 1999 Gregorian (1 Chet 531 Nanakshahi) and aligns with the Gregorian calendar[cite: 13]. The era (1 Chet 1 Nanakshahi) marks the birth of the 1st Guru, Nanak Dev Ji, in the Punjab in 1469[cite: 13]. Although influenced by the Hindu Solar Calendar, the Sikh Organization affirms that Nanakshahi dates are fixed relative to the Gregorian calendar[cite: 13].

Baramaha - The Twelve Months of the Sikh Year

Baramaha is the name of a poetic composition in the Sri Guru Granth Sahib that mentions the twelve Indian months[cite: 13]. Guru Arjan Dev Ji composed a well-known Baramaha in Raag Majh, which is often recited in Gurudwaras on Sangrand[cite: 13]. Guru Nanak Dev Ji also composed a Baramaha in Raag Tukhari[cite: 13]. These compositions use the changing moods of nature throughout the year to symbolize spiritual emotions and yearnings of the soul[cite: 13].
