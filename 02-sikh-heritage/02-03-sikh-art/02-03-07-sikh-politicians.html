Sikh Politicians & Statesmen

🏛 Statesmen

Sardar Baldev Singh (1902–1961)

India’s first Defence Minister

Baldev Singh was born on 11 July 1902 at the village of Dumna in Ropar district, Punjab, the son of the industrialist Sir Inder Singh. Educated at Ambala and Khalsa College, Amritsar, he joined his father’s steel business and became a director of the firm. He entered politics in 1937, when he won a seat in the Punjab provincial assembly as a Panthic Party candidate, worked closely with Master Tara Singh and the Shiromani Akali Dal, and in 1942 became a minister in the Punjab government.

In the negotiations of 1946–47 he represented Sikh and Punjabi interests, and on 15 August 1947 he became independent India’s first Defence Minister in Jawaharlal Nehru’s cabinet, serving until 1952. He led the ministry during the first Kashmir war of 1947–48 and helped manage security and relief during the violence of Partition. He was elected to the first Lok Sabha in 1952 and again in 1957, and died in Delhi on 29 June 1961; he was cremated in his native village with full military honours.

Dr Manmohan Singh (1932–2024)

India’s first Sikh Prime Minister

Manmohan Singh was born on 26 September 1932 at Gah in western Punjab (now in Pakistan). After degrees from Panjab University (BA 1952, MA 1954) he studied economics at St John’s College, Cambridge, where he won the Wright’s Prize and the Adam Smith Prize (1956), and took a DPhil at Nuffield College, Oxford. He taught at Panjab University and the Delhi School of Economics, and went on to serve as Chief Economic Adviser, Governor of the Reserve Bank of India and Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission. He received the Padma Vibhushan in 1987.

As Finance Minister from 1991 to 1996 under P. V. Narasimha Rao, he led the economic reforms that opened India’s economy, and was named Finance Minister of the Year by Euromoney (1993) and Asiamoney. He was a member of the Rajya Sabha from 1991 to 2024 and Leader of the Opposition there from 1998 to 2004. On 22 May 2004 he became Prime Minister, the first Sikh and the first member of a minority faith to hold the office, and served two full terms until May 2014. He died in New Delhi on 26 December 2024, aged 92.
