Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji
The Eternal Living Guru of the Sikhs
Sab Sikhan Ko Hukam Hai Guru Manyo Granth
 By the order of the Timeless Lord, the sacred scripture is the ultimate spiritual authority for all time.

Why is Guru Granth Sahib Ji Considered the Eternal Guru?

Key Takeaways

  • In 1708, Guru Gobind Singh Ji formally bestowed Guruship upon the Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, ending human physical succession.
  • Sikh theology posits that the true Guru is the divine wisdom and word (Shabad Guru), which is indestructible and timeless.
  • The text was directly compiled and authenticated by the Sikh Gurus themselves, preserving the original divine revelations without alteration.
  • It contains an inclusive, universal message featuring hymns from Sikh Gurus, Hindu and Muslim saints, and devout bards.

The designation of the Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji as the eternal, living Guru of the Sikhs is a defining principle of Sikhism. This status was formally bestowed upon the holy scripture in 1708 by the tenth human Guru, Guru Gobind Singh Ji, marking the end of the line of human Gurus and establishing the scripture as the ultimate spiritual authority for all time.

1. Direct Divine Command (Gurgaddi)

In October 1708, shortly before his passing at Nanded (Hazur Sahib), Guru Gobind Singh Ji formally passed the lineage of Guruship (Gurgaddi) to the sacred scripture (Adi Granth). He declared to the assembled Sikhs:

"Aagya Bhei Akal Ki Tabhi Chalayo Panth, Sab Sikhan Ko Hukam Hai Guru Manyo Granth."
(By the order of the Timeless Lord, the Panth was established. All Sikhs are commanded to accept the Granth as their Guru.)

From that moment forward, the physical succession of human Gurus ended, and the scripture was anointed as the eternal, living embodiment of the Gurus' divine wisdom.

2. The Word is the Guru (Shabad Guru)

In Sikh theology, the true Guru has never been the physical body of a human being, but rather the divine revelation and wisdom (Shabad) contained within them. Throughout the scripture, the Sikh Gurus themselves emphasized that the divine word is the true spiritual guide:

  • Guru Nanak Dev Ji stated: "The Word is the Guru, and the mind tuned to it is the disciple." (Ang 943)
  • Guru Ram Das Ji wrote: "The Word (Bani) is the Guru, and the Guru is the Word; within the Word, all nectars reside." (Ang 982)

Because physical human bodies are mortal, a human master eventually passes away. However, divine truth and wisdom (Gurbani) are eternal, timeless, and indestructible.

3. Preserving Divine Truth Without Alteration

Unlike many historical religious texts that were compiled decades or centuries after the passing of their founders, the original text of the Guru Granth Sahib Ji was directly compiled and verified by the Sikh Gurus themselves:

  • Compilation of the Adi Granth (1604): The fifth master, Guru Arjan Dev Ji, meticulously collected, authenticated, and compiled the divine hymns of the first five Gurus, along with the writings of Hindu and Muslim saints. He installed it in Harmandir Sahib (The Golden Temple).
  • Final Recension (1706): Guru Gobind Singh Ji dictated the full scripture from memory at Damdama Sahib, adding the hymns of the ninth master, Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji, and sealing the canon so that no line could ever be added, removed, or altered.

Because it contains the original, authentic divine revelations without historical distortion, it serves as an immutable, eternal guide.

4. Universal and Inclusive Message

The Guru Granth Sahib Ji is unique among world scriptures because it is not restricted to the writings of a single prophet or religion. It includes the divine compositions of:

Contributors Details
Six Sikh Gurus Guru Nanak Dev Ji, Guru Angad Dev Ji, Guru Amar Das Ji, Guru Ram Das Ji, Guru Arjan Dev Ji, and Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji.
15 Bhagats (Saints) Holy figures from Hindu, Muslim, and lower-caste backgrounds (such as Kabir, Ravidas, Namdev, and Sheikh Farid).
11 Bhatts & 4 Devout Followers Inspirational bards and devoted followers of the Sikh Gurus.

Because its core message transcends geographical, social, caste, and religious boundaries—emphasizing love, humility, truth, and the oneness of humanity under One God (Ik Onkar)—its spiritual guidance remains universally relevant across all eras and cultures.

5. Practical Reverence as a Living Guru

Sikhs do not treat the Guru Granth Sahib Ji merely as a book, but with the utmost reverence due to a living sovereign:

  • It is seated on an elevated throne (Prakash) under a canopy (Chanani) in every Gurdwara.
  • A royal whisk (Chaur Sahib) is waved over it as a symbol of spiritual sovereignty.
  • At the end of the day, it is respectfully retired to a dedicated resting room (Sukh Asan).
  • Spiritual decisions and daily guidance (Hukamnama) are sought by opening the scripture to receive a random divine command for the day.

By transitioning spiritual authority to the Guru Granth Sahib Ji (the Shabad or Divine Word) and temporal authority to the Guru Khalsa Panth (the collective body of initiated Sikhs), Guru Gobind Singh Ji ensured that Sikhism would remain free from the potential corruption, hereditary claims, or division that often accompany human religious leadership.
