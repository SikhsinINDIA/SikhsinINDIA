Meaning of Ik Onkar (ੴ)

Key Takeaways

  • Ik Onkar (ੴ) is the central foundational concept of Sikhism and opens the sacred scripture, Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji.
  • It consists of three components: Ik (1 - Absolute Oneness), Om/On (Primal Sound), and Kar (Eternity & Continuity).
  • Philosophically, it asserts non-duality, both transcendent and immanent divine nature, and the universal equality of all human beings.
  • The symbol ੴ features the numeral 1 (੧) at the front to remove all ambiguity about the singularity of the Divine.

Ik Onkar (ੴ) is the central foundational concept of Sikhism. It appears at the very beginning of the Sikh sacred scripture, the Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, as the opening phrase of the Mool Mantar (the primary hymn of faith).

Literal Breakdown and Meaning

The phrase is composed of three parts:

  • Ik (੧): The numeral One. It emphasizes absolute oneness and singularity—there is only One Supreme Power, without any rival, partner, or second.
  • Om / On: The divine sound or primal vibration from which all existence originates.
  • Kar: A suffix denoting continuity and eternity. It signifies that the Divine Source is all-pervading, unmoving, and continuously manifesting throughout creation.
"There is One Supreme Being / One Universal Creator."

Core Philosophical Significance

Non-Duality & Divine Oneness

Unlike polytheistic frameworks or dualistic views of good versus evil, Ik Onkar asserts that everything in the universe emanates from a single, indivisible Source.

Immanence and Transcendence

It signifies that God is both transcendent (existing beyond the physical universe) and immanent (dwelling within every living being, object, and particle of creation).

Universal Equality

Because the same Divine Light (Jot) resides within everyone, Ik Onkar forms the theological foundation for Sikhism's rejection of the caste system, gender inequality, and religious elitism. All human beings are equal children of the same One Creator.

Symbolism (The Gurmukhi Symbol ੴ)

The symbol ੴ is used widely in Sikh art, gurdwaras, and daily life. The prominent inclusion of the numeral 1 (੧) at the front was explicitly chosen by Guru Nanak Dev Ji to ensure there is no ambiguity: the Divine is strictly One, beyond form, gender, nation, or religious label.
