What is the Meaning of Nitnem?

Key Takeaways

  • Nitnem combines Nit (daily) and Nem (discipline/covenant), referring to the prescribed daily recitation of Gurbani hymns.
  • It forms the spiritual foundation of a Sikh's life, cultivating humility, inner strength, focus, and devotion.
  • The standard schedule is divided across three times: Amrit Vela (morning), Rehras Sahib (sunset), and Sohila Sahib (bedtime).
  • Reciting Nitnem is mandatory for Amritdhari Sikhs and strongly encouraged for all Sikhs to keep spiritual awareness integrated into daily living.

Nitnem (literally translating to "daily routine" or "daily discipline") refers to the designated collection of Sikh hymns (Gurbani) that Sikhs are prescribed to read or recite every day.

Core Meaning and Purpose

The word is composed of two words:

  • Nit: Daily or always
  • Nem: Rule, discipline, or spiritual covenant

Together, Nitnem forms the spiritual foundation of a Sikh's daily life. It serves as a continuous reminder of divine truth, cultivating humility, inner strength, focus, and devotion from sunrise to bedtime.

The Daily Nitnem Schedule

The standard Nitnem prescribed by the Sikh Rehat Maryada (Sikh Code of Conduct) is divided across three times of the day:

1. Amrit Vela (Early Morning / Before Dawn)

Sikhs rise early during the ambrosial hours (Amrit Vela) to bathe, meditate on Naam, and recite five morning prayers:

  • Japji Sahib: Composed by Guru Nanak Dev Ji; lays out the core theological foundation of Sikhism.
  • Jaap Sahib: Composed by Guru Gobind Singh Ji; describes the infinite attributes and formless nature of the Divine.
  • Tav-Prasad Savaiye: Composed by Guru Gobind Singh Ji; emphasizes that devotion and love for God are superior to empty rituals or worldly power.
  • Chaupai Sahib: Composed by Guru Gobind Singh Ji; a prayer seeking divine protection, strength, and spiritual elevation.
  • Anand Sahib: Composed by Guru Amar Das Ji; expresses the state of supreme bliss and realization achieved through the Guru's wisdom.

2. Rehras Sahib (Evening / Sunset)

Recited at sunset when the day's work is concluding, Rehras Sahib brings gratitude, emotional balance, and peace after a busy day. It includes compositions from several Sikh Gurus.

3. Sohila Sahib (Night / Before Bed)

Recited immediately before sleeping, Sohila Sahib (also known as Kirtan Sohila) is a soothing prayer that eliminates fear, protects the mind, and reminds the individual of the soul's ultimate journey and union with the Creator.

Time of Day Prescribed Prayers Spiritual Focus
Amrit Vela (Early Morning) Japji Sahib, Jaap Sahib, Tav-Prasad Savaiye, Chaupai Sahib, Anand Sahib Theological foundation, divine attributes, protection, and supreme bliss.
Evening (Sunset) Rehras Sahib Gratitude, emotional balance, and peace after the day's work.
Night (Before Bed) Sohila Sahib (Kirtan Sohila) Eliminating fear, protecting the mind, and soul's union with Creator.

Philosophical Significance

For an initiated (Amritdhari) Sikh, reciting daily Nitnem is a mandatory vow. However, all Sikhs are encouraged to practice Nitnem as a way to integrate spiritual awareness into their everyday routine, ensuring that worldly responsibilities do not overshadow moral integrity and devotion to God.
