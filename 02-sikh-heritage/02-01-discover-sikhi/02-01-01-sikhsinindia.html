Sikhs in India – Legacy & Contributions

Sikhs in India form a dynamic and deeply respected community, representing courage, equality, and devotion. Founded by Guru Nanak Dev Ji in the 15th century, Sikhism stands as one of the youngest yet most impactful religions in the world. It teaches oneness of God, honest living, selfless service (Seva), and equality of all humanity.

Population and Presence

As of 2025, the estimated Sikh population in India is approximately 22–25 million (about 2.0% of the country’s total population). Most Sikhs live in Punjab, where they make up around 58% of the state’s population, with thriving communities in Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Jammu & Kashmir. India remains the spiritual and cultural home of the Sikh faith.

Spiritual Heritage

The cornerstone of Sikhism is the Guru Granth Sahib Ji — the eternal Guru and living scripture that guides Sikhs toward truth, humility, and remembrance of the Divine (Naam). The teachings of the ten Gurus emphasize unity, compassion, and moral integrity. Daily prayers such as Japji Sahib and Rehras Sahib nurture spiritual discipline and connection with God.

Gurudwaras – Halls of Service and Faith

Across India, Gurudwaras are open to everyone, offering peace, guidance, and Langar (free community meals) for all, regardless of caste or creed. The Golden Temple (Sri Harmandir Sahib) in Amritsar is the most revered shrine, symbolizing equality and divine presence. Thousands of other Gurudwaras continue this sacred tradition across India.

Tradition and Identity

The Sikh identity is represented through the Five Ks (Kesh, Kara, Kanga, Kachera, Kirpan) — each carrying deep spiritual significance. Sikhs celebrate major festivals such as Gurpurab, Baisakhi, and Hola Mohalla, honoring faith, bravery, and unity.

Contributions to India

Sikhs have made exceptional contributions to India’s freedom struggle, defense forces, agriculture, sports, business, and philanthropy. Their values of courage, discipline, and compassion embody the true spirit of India.

Our Mission

Sikhs in India is dedicated to sharing, connecting, and preserving Sikh heritage. Through digital initiatives, education, and community engagement, we aim to spread the timeless messages of the Gurus — truth, equality, and universal brotherhood — to inspire present and future generations.
