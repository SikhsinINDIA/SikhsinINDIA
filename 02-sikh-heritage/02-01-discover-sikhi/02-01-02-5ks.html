The Five K's – Identity and Conduct

In bestowing a distinct character and identity Guru Gobind Singh Ji let it be known that the artefacts that he was offering the Khalsa were not mere whims of fashion but were integral to the Khalsa Rehat and Life[cite: 3]. His command to the Khalsa was, "Rehat pyari mujh ko, Sikh pyaraae nahin"[cite: 3]. It was the Sikh's life and conduct that were important; calling oneself Sikh is not enough to become a Sikh![cite: 3]

Guru Ji commanded that the Khalsa will be known for they will bear the stamp of the Guru by wearing on their person the Five Kakaar: Kesh, Kanga, Kirpan, Kachera, Kara[cite: 3].

Kesh

All who wish to be known as the Khalsa will not allow the scissors or the knife to touch their hair[cite: 3]. The hair will be worn unshorn and tied in a knot to keep them manageable[cite: 3].

Kanga

Kanga was to be the instrument of cleansing[cite: 3]. A Sikh should wear the Kanga in his/her hair and regularly comb his/her hair to keep them clean and tidy[cite: 3].

Kirpan

The Kirpan is a small sword that the Khalsa wears diagonally on his torso[cite: 3]. It reminds the Khalsa of their responsibilities and ability to defend themselves and others[cite: 3].

Kachera

The garment of modesty, ensuring dignity and readiness for battle[cite: 3]. Designed with a drawstring to stay secure[cite: 3].

Kara

The Kara is a constant reminder of Waheguru's presence, worn on the most active hand to encourage mindfulness and righteous action[cite: 3].
