Japji Sahib Ji: The Foundation of Sikh Prayer

About Japji Sahib

Japji Sahib is the first bani of Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji (Ang 1–8) and was composed by Guru Nanak Dev Ji, the founder of Sikhism.

It opens with the Mool Mantar, the foundational statement of Sikh belief in One Creator, and then sets out the essence of the Guru's teaching in a compact, poetic form.

Because so much of Gurmat is introduced here, Japji Sahib is often described as the key to understanding the whole of Guru Granth Sahib Ji.

When it is recited

Japji Sahib is the first bani of the morning Nitnem and is traditionally recited in the early hours of the day (Amritvela), before the day's work begins.

It is also one of the five banis recited at Amrit Sanchar, the Khalsa initiation ceremony, and many Sikhs read or listen to it whenever they need clarity and direction.

How it is arranged

  • The Mool Mantar, beginning with Ik Onkar (One Creator).
  • An opening salok: “Aad sach, jugaad sach…” (True in the beginning, true through the ages).
  • 38 pauris (stanzas) that develop the Guru's teaching step by step.
  • A closing salok, “Pavan guru, paani pita…”, describing air as the Guru, water as the father and the earth as the great mother.

Why Japji Sahib matters

  • Living by Hukam: the very first pauri teaches that the way to truth is to walk in harmony with the Divine Will (“Hukam rajaai chalnaa”).
  • Beyond ritual: it explains that silence, fasting, cleverness or outward ritual cannot win the Divine; truthful living and the Name (Naam) can.
  • Listening and believing: the pauris on Suniai (listening) and Mannai (accepting the Word) describe how attention to the Guru's word changes a person from within.
  • The five realms: pauris 34–37 describe spiritual growth through Dharam Khand, Gian Khand, Saram Khand, Karam Khand and Sach Khand.
  • Humility: it reminds us that creation is vast beyond counting, so no one should claim to know its limits.
  • A practical character: pauri 38 compares spiritual life to a goldsmith's workshop, with self-control as the furnace and patience as the goldsmith.

The core message

Japji Sahib teaches that there is One Creator present in all, that truth is reached by living truthfully and accepting the Divine Will, and that grace comes to those who remember the Name with love.

Read daily, it gives the mind a steady starting point: gratitude, humility and a clear sense of purpose for the day ahead.

Frequently asked questions

Who wrote Japji Sahib?

Guru Nanak Dev Ji, the first Sikh Guru. It appears at the very beginning of Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, on Ang 1 to 8.

How long does it take to recite Japji Sahib?

Usually about 15 to 25 minutes, depending on the pace of recitation.

What is the Mool Mantar?

The opening lines of Japji Sahib, beginning “Ik Onkar”, which describe the One Creator: Truth, the Creator, without fear, without enmity, timeless, beyond birth, self-existent, realised through the Guru's grace.
