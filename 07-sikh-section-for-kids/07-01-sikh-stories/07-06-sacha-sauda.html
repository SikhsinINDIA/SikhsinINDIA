Stories of Guru Nanak Dev Ji
The True Bargain by Guru Nanak Dev Ji

1. A Young Guru with a Big Heart

Guru Nanak Dev Ji was just a child when his father gave him 20 rupees and told him to go to the market to do a profitable trade — a good bargain (Sauda)[cite: 18]. His father wanted him to learn the ways of business[cite: 18].

2. On the Way to the Market

Guru Nanak Ji began walking toward the market but soon came across a group of hungry, poor sadhus (holy men) sitting under a tree[cite: 18]. They had not eaten for days[cite: 18].

3. The Realization

Young Nanak looked at them and said, “They are also children of Waheguru[cite: 18]. Feeding them is better than buying goods to sell for profit.”[cite: 18]

4. The True Bargain – Sacha Sauda

He used all the 20 rupees to buy food and served it to the hungry sadhus with love and respect[cite: 18]. When he returned home, he told his father, “I did a Sacha Sauda – a true bargain.”[cite: 18]

5. A Lesson for the World

Though his father was initially upset, this story later became one of the most famous examples of Guru Nanak’s teachings[cite: 18]. Even today, Gurdwara Sacha Sauda stands at that spot in Shekhupura, Pakistan[cite: 18].

💡 The Message

  • 🔶 The best profit is earned through kindness and helping others[cite: 18].
  • 🟠 Selfless service is the foundation of Sikhi[cite: 18].
  • 🟡 Feeding the hungry and serving humanity is the truest business of all[cite: 18].

📚 Moral of the Story

“Sacha Sauda is not found in markets, but in acts of compassion and truth.”[cite: 18]
